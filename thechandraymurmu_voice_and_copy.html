01

Tone of Voice

Four attributes that define how thechandraymurmu sounds — and just as importantly, what it doesn't sound like.

Expert, not arrogant

You know your craft deeply. But you never talk over people's heads or make them feel small for not knowing something.

"Here's how I'd approach this for you."
"As an experienced full-stack engineer..."
Warm, not gushing

Friendly and human — but not over-the-top. No "Amazing! Fantastic! So excited to work with you!" every other sentence.

"Let's figure out what actually works for you."
"We are SO passionate about your success!!"
Clear, not dumbed down

Plain language is a sign of respect, not a sign that you think your audience is stupid. Short words beat long ones.

"I build websites that bring in clients."
"I deliver end-to-end digital transformation."
Honest, not salesy

You say what's true — even if it's "this might not be the right fit." Trust builds faster with honesty than with hype.

"Here's what you actually need (and what you don't)."
"Our premium solution maximises your ROI potential."
02

Writing Principles

Five rules to follow every time you write — for your site, your social posts, your README, or a client email.

01

Write to one person

Never write to "companies" or "users." Write to one person — Sam the founder, Priya the freelancer. Use "you" and "I", never "clients" or "our team." It should feel like a message, not a brochure.

02

Lead with the outcome, not the process

Your audience cares about what they'll have, not how you'll build it. Start with "You'll get a website that brings in leads" before "I use Next.js, Tailwind, and a headless CMS."

03

Short sentences are a superpower

If a sentence has more than 20 words, cut it in two. Short sentences build confidence. Long sentences signal uncertainty. Punchy writing = someone who knows what they're doing.

04

Make every CTA a next step, not a pressure move

Replace "Buy Now" with "Start a conversation". Replace "Limited spots available!" with "When you're ready, I'm here." Low pressure CTAs convert better for a personal brand built on trust.

05

Show, don't just tell

Instead of "I'm reliable," say "Every project I've shipped has landed on time." Instead of "I care about quality," show a project and walk through one decision you made for the user's benefit.

★

Recommended

Speaks directly to the audience's ambition. Optimistic and motivating — positions the brand as the catalyst for their next step.

Tone by Context

The brand voice stays consistent — but the register shifts depending on where and why you're writing.

Hero headline · Website
"Your idea deserves a real home online. Let's build it."
Inspiring · Bold · Direct. One sentence. No jargon.
LinkedIn Bio
"Developer & creator building websites, SaaS tools, and open-source projects for startups and small businesses. I ship things that work."
Professional · Confident · Clear credentials upfront.
Twitter / X Bio
"building for the web · open source · helping small biz show up online · @thechandraymurmu"
Casual · Lowercase energy · Brief. Keywords natural.
GitHub README Intro
"Hey — I'm Chandray. I build open-source tools and web products. Everything here is free to use, fork, and improve."
Developer-native · Generous · No marketing fluff.
Error Message · SaaS product
"Something went wrong on our end — not yours. Try refreshing, or drop me a message if it keeps happening."
Human · Blame-free · Offers a path forward. No "Error 500."
Onboarding / Success Message
"You're in. Let's get your project off the ground — I'll walk you through everything step by step."
Warm · Reassuring · Makes the user feel supported, not alone.
